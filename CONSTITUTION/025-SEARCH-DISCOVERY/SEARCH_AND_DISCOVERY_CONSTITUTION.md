# FEMC Search and Discovery Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define search and discovery as ways of navigating the family memory graph.

## 1. Search Is Not the Source of Truth

Search indexes, rankings, embeddings, and retrieval structures are derived representations.

The canonical family record remains authoritative.

## 2. Contextual Discovery

Discovery should allow movement through:

- people;
- relationships;
- events;
- time;
- memories;
- media;
- albums;
- stories;
- legacy.

## 3. Natural Language

Future search may allow people to ask questions naturally.

The answer must remain grounded in authorized family information.

## 4. Permission-Aware Retrieval

Search must not reveal information simply because it matches a query.

Authorization remains a prerequisite for meaningful retrieval.

## 5. Uncertainty

When search cannot establish an answer confidently, FEMC should communicate uncertainty rather than inventing a result.

## 6. Historical Search

Discovery should support old and approximate information without requiring false precision.

## 7. AI Search

AI may summarize or connect retrieved information.

AI-generated interpretation must remain distinguishable from canonical family facts.

## 8. Provider Independence

The conceptual search experience should not depend permanently on one search or AI provider.

## 9. Principle

Search should help families discover meaning, not merely locate records.
