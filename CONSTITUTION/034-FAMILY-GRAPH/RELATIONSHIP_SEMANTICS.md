# FEMC Relationship Semantics

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Protect the meaning of relationships as the family graph evolves.

## Relationship Dimensions

A meaningful relationship may include:

- type;
- participants;
- direction where relevant;
- temporal context;
- provenance;
- confidence or status;
- family-specific terminology.

## 1. Relationship Is Not an Assumption

The system should not create a relationship solely from weak signals.

## 2. Family-Confirmed Relationships

Where a relationship is confirmed by an authorized family participant, it may become canonical according to the appropriate governance rules.

## 3. Suggested Relationships

AI or automated systems may suggest relationships.

Suggestions must remain distinguishable from confirmed relationships.

## 4. Historical Relationships

A relationship may have changed over time.

The historical record should not be destroyed simply because the current state is different.

## 5. Cultural Language

Different cultures may use different terms for similar relationships.

Localized language must not corrupt the underlying semantic model.

## 6. Privacy

Relationship information can itself be sensitive.

Access must be controlled appropriately.

## 7. Principle

FEMC should preserve both the existence of relationships and the uncertainty or history surrounding them.
