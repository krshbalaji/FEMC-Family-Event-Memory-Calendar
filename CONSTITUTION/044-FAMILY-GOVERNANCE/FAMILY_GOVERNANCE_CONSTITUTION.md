# FEMC Family Governance Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define governance for decisions that affect shared family information, responsibilities, permissions, stewardship, and long-term preservation.

## 1. Family Governance Is Contextual

Different families may organize responsibility differently.

FEMC should support family-defined governance within platform safety and policy boundaries.

## 2. Governance Is Not Ownership

A steward or administrator may manage family information without owning the family's memories.

## 3. Shared Decisions

Certain actions may require appropriate participation from more than one authorized person.

## 4. Stewardship

Future FEMC capabilities may support long-term family stewards responsible for preservation and continuity.

## 5. Delegation

Governance responsibilities may be delegated within explicit boundaries.

## 6. Conflict

Family disagreements may occur.

FEMC should avoid becoming an arbiter of family disputes unless a clearly governed capability explicitly requires it.

## 7. Historical Integrity

Governance changes must not silently rewrite family history.

## 8. AI

AI may assist governance workflows but must not become the final authority over family relationships or rights.

## 9. Principle

Governance should help families manage shared information responsibly without turning FEMC into the family's judge.
