# FEMC Audit and Accountability Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define how FEMC maintains understandable accountability for important actions affecting family information, permissions, security, AI behavior, and platform governance.

## 1. Accountability Is a Trust Property

Families should be able to trust that important actions can be understood and, where appropriate, investigated.

## 2. Audit Is Not Surveillance

Auditability must not become an excuse for collecting unnecessary information about family members.

Only information justified by security, integrity, governance, or operational needs should be retained.

## 3. Material Actions

Future audit architecture should consider material actions such as:

- permission changes;
- important sharing actions;
- deletion;
- restoration;
- administrative changes;
- significant data migrations;
- security events;
- material AI actions affecting family information.

## 4. Provenance

Important records should retain sufficient provenance to establish:

- what happened;
- when it happened;
- relevant actor or system;
- affected context;
- outcome.

## 5. Tamper Awareness

Audit information should be protected against inappropriate alteration.

## 6. Family Context

Audit records must not be confused with family history.

An audit event describes system activity; it does not automatically become a family memory.

## 7. AI Accountability

Where AI materially affects family information, appropriate records should support understanding of:

- the action;
- relevant authorization;
- whether output was generated or inferred;
- whether human confirmation occurred.

## 8. Retention

Audit retention should be proportionate to its purpose and applicable requirements.

## 9. Principle

The system should be accountable without becoming intrusive.
