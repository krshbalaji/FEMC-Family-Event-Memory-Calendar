# FEMC Architectural Principles

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## 1. One Family Reality

FEMC should maintain one coherent conceptual family record.

## 2. Canonical Meaning

Each core concept has one canonical meaning.

## 3. Relationships Are First-Class

The connections between people, events, memories, and time carry meaning and must not be treated as incidental metadata.

## 4. Privacy Is Architectural

Privacy must influence boundaries and information flows from the beginning.

## 5. Trust Is a Product Capability

Permission, visibility, sharing, auditability, and user control are part of the product architecture.

## 6. AI Is Contextual

AI receives only the context necessary and authorized for its task.

## 7. Human Confirmation Matters

AI inference must not silently become historical fact.

## 8. Technology Is Replaceable

The domain model must survive changes in frameworks, databases, cloud providers, AI providers, and interface technologies.

## 9. Complexity Requires Justification

No architectural complexity should be introduced without a demonstrated need.

## 10. Evolution Must Be Safe

Historical family information must survive platform evolution.

## 11. Export Is Part of Architecture

A family must not be trapped inside FEMC.

## 12. Scale Without Fragmentation

Scaling the implementation must not require fragmenting the conceptual family model unnecessarily.

## 13. Reversibility

Where future requirements are uncertain, prefer decisions that preserve future choices.

## 14. Observability and Accountability

Production systems must eventually make important failures, changes, and access patterns understandable and auditable.

## 15. Ten-Year Test

Every foundational architectural decision should be evaluated against the question:

> Will this still make sense if FEMC is preserving family memories ten years from now?
