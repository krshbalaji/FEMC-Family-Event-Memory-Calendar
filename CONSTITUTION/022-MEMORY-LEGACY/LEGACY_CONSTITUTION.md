# FEMC Legacy Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define legacy as the long-term continuity of family knowledge, memory, relationships, and stories across generations.

## 1. Legacy Is a Core Outcome

Legacy is not merely an export function.

It is the ability for family meaning to continue beyond individual users, devices, and product generations.

## 2. Generational Continuity

Future generations should be able to understand, where appropriately authorized:

- who people were;
- how they were connected;
- what happened;
- what memories mattered;
- what stories were preserved.

## 3. Preservation Before Presentation

Long-term preservation must not depend entirely on today's user interface.

## 4. Human Meaning

Legacy should preserve context and meaning, not merely raw files.

## 5. Controlled Access

Legacy information must remain subject to appropriate privacy, permission, and governance rules.

## 6. Generational Handover

Future capabilities may support controlled transfer or delegation of family stewardship.

Such mechanisms must be deliberate and auditable.

## 7. AI and Legacy

AI may help future generations discover and understand historical material.

It must distinguish source history from generated interpretation.

## 8. Historical Integrity

Legacy systems must preserve the difference between:

- confirmed history;
- family stories;
- uncertainty;
- later interpretation.

## 9. Technology Independence

Legacy must survive replacement of:

- storage systems;
- AI providers;
- interfaces;
- infrastructure;
- implementation teams.

## 10. Final Principle

FEMC should help a family leave behind more than files.

It should help preserve a comprehensible family story.
