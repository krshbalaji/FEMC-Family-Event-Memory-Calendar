# FEMC Event and Time Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define Events and Time as foundational dimensions of the FEMC family model.

## 1. Event Is a Domain Concept

An event represents something meaningful that occurs, occurred, or is intended to occur in a family context.

A calendar entry is only one presentation of an event.

## 2. Event Context

An event may connect to:

- people;
- relationships;
- family contexts;
- memories;
- media;
- celebrations;
- communication;
- places;
- legacy.

## 3. Event States

Future product design may need to distinguish concepts such as:

- planned;
- scheduled;
- occurring;
- completed;
- cancelled;
- historical;
- recurring.

These states must not erase historical meaning.

## 4. Time Is Not Only a Date

FEMC should conceptually support:

- exact moments;
- dates;
- date ranges;
- approximate dates;
- recurring periods;
- relative time;
- unknown time;
- historical time.

## 5. No False Precision

If a family knows that something happened "around 1985", FEMC must not force a fabricated exact date.

## 6. Time Zones

The underlying event meaning should be distinguishable from how a date and time are presented to a person in a particular location.

## 7. Recurrence

Recurring events should preserve the distinction between:

- the recurring pattern;
- individual occurrences;
- memories associated with specific occurrences.

## 8. Historical Events

Historical events may exist without having been created in FEMC at the time they occurred.

## 9. AI Boundary

AI may help interpret, find, or organize events.

It must not invent dates or event history.

## 10. Principle

The calendar is a window into family time.

It is not the owner of time itself.
