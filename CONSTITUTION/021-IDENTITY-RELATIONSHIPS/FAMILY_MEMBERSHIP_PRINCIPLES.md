# FEMC Family Membership Principles

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## 1. Membership Is Not Ownership

Participation in a family context does not automatically mean ownership of all family information.

## 2. Membership Is Not Permission

Being a family member does not automatically grant unrestricted access.

Permissions remain contextual.

## 3. Membership Can Evolve

Family participation may change over time.

Historical membership should not be silently rewritten.

## 4. Multiple Contexts

The model should allow a person to participate in more than one family context where appropriate.

## 5. Delegated Participation

Future capabilities may support guardians, delegated contributors, or other roles without changing the underlying person model.

## 6. Children and Dependent Participants

Future product and governance models should consider situations where a person cannot independently manage all aspects of their participation.

## 7. Legacy Participation

Family membership may have relevance to long-term legacy and generational preservation.

## 8. User Experience

Family terminology should be understandable and culturally adaptable.

## 9. Data Integrity

Removing participation from a current family context must not automatically erase historical memories or events involving the person.

## 10. Constitutional Test

Membership should answer:

> In what family context does this person participate, and what does that participation mean?

It should not attempt to answer every question through one field or role.
