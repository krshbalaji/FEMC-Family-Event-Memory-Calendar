# FEMC Identity and Relationship Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define the conceptual foundation for identity, people, family membership, and relationships within FEMC.

## 1. Person Is a Core Domain Concept

A person represents a human within the family context.

A person must not be reduced to an account or login credential.

## 2. Identity Is Distinct

Identity establishes how someone participates in FEMC.

A person may have identity-related representations without the identity itself being the complete definition of the person.

## 3. Family Membership Is Contextual

A person's participation in a family context is distinct from the person's existence as a human.

The model must support people participating in multiple legitimate family contexts where appropriate.

## 4. Relationships Are First-Class

Relationships are meaningful domain information.

They must not be represented only as incidental profile fields.

## 5. Relationships Can Change

Family relationships may change over time.

The system must be capable of preserving historical meaning rather than assuming today's relationship state was always true.

## 6. Relationship Types

The platform should support canonical relationship concepts while allowing family-appropriate terminology and localization.

## 7. No Universal Family Shape

FEMC must not impose one rigid family hierarchy.

It should support different family structures without weakening conceptual clarity.

## 8. Identity and Privacy

Knowing a person's identity does not automatically grant access to every family relationship, memory, or event involving that person.

## 9. AI Boundary

AI may help interpret or navigate existing relationships but must not invent family relationships.

AI suggestions about relationships must remain distinguishable from confirmed family information.

## 10. Ten-Year Principle

The identity and relationship model must remain understandable if a family has accumulated generations of history.
