# FEMC Consent and Access Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define the conceptual relationship between consent, authorization, visibility, and access.

## 1. Consent Is Not the Entire Permission Model

Consent may be one basis for processing or sharing.

Access control also depends on:

- role;
- relationship;
- purpose;
- family context;
- resource;
- applicable governance.

## 2. Meaningful Consent

Where consent is required, it should be understandable and reasonably specific.

## 3. No Blanket Assumption

Agreeing to use FEMC does not mean agreeing to every future use of family information.

## 4. Granularity

Where appropriate, people should be able to make meaningful choices about:

- sharing;
- visibility;
- AI assistance;
- external integrations;
- communication;
- preservation.

## 5. Withdrawal

Where applicable, withdrawal of permission should have predictable consequences.

## 6. Historical Integrity

Changing access should not automatically erase the historical existence of an event or memory.

## 7. AI

Consent or authorization for ordinary FEMC access does not automatically grant unrestricted AI access to all family information.

## 8. Children and Delegation

Future designs must consider situations where authorization involves guardianship or delegated authority.

## 9. Principle

Access should be understandable, purposeful, bounded, and reviewable.
