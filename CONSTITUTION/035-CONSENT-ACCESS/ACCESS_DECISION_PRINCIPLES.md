# FEMC Access Decision Principles

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Access Decision Context

Conceptually evaluate:

```text
WHO
 ↓
WANTS TO DO WHAT
 ↓
TO WHICH RESOURCE
 ↓
IN WHICH FAMILY CONTEXT
 ↓
FOR WHAT PURPOSE
 ↓
UNDER WHICH PERMISSION
```

## 1. Least Privilege

Grant only the access required for the intended purpose.

## 2. Contextual Access

Access should consider family context rather than relying solely on generic roles.

## 3. Resource Sensitivity

Different information may require different access treatment.

## 4. Action Sensitivity

Viewing, editing, sharing, deleting, exporting, and delegating are not equivalent actions.

## 5. AI Access

AI retrieval and processing are actions that require authorization.

## 6. Administrative Access

Operational administration must remain distinct from family ownership.

## 7. Auditability

Material access changes should support appropriate accountability.

## 8. User Understanding

Important access decisions should communicate meaningful consequences.

## 9. Failure

When authorization cannot be established reliably, safe denial is preferable to silent exposure.

## 10. Principle

Convenience must never be the reason a trust boundary disappears.
