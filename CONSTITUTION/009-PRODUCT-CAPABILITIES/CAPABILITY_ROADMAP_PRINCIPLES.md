# FEMC Capability Roadmap Principles

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Strategic Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Principle 1 — Foundation Before Intelligence

Build trustworthy family, relationship, event, memory, media, permission, and preservation foundations before depending on advanced AI.

## Principle 2 — Connection Before Expansion

Strengthen relationships between existing capabilities before adding large numbers of new capabilities.

## Principle 3 — Depth Before Breadth

A small number of deeply integrated capabilities is preferable to a large number of disconnected features.

## Principle 4 — Preserve Optionality

Future capabilities should not require today's technology choices to remain permanent.

## Principle 5 — Avoid Engagement Optimization

Capability success should be measured by family value, not screen time.

## Principle 6 — Protect the Archive

New experiences must not put historical family information at unnecessary risk.

## Principle 7 — AI Is Progressive

Introduce AI where it creates clear value, beginning with assistance and moving toward contextual intelligence only as trust, data quality, and governance mature.

## Principle 8 — Accessibility Is Core

Capabilities should be designed so that age, language, ability, and technical familiarity do not unnecessarily exclude family members.

## Principle 9 — Global Family Reality

The conceptual capability model must support different family structures, cultures, traditions, and communication patterns without hard-coding one family's assumptions.

## Principle 10 — Capability Retirement Is Healthy

FEMC should be willing to retire capabilities that no longer serve families.

Retirement of a feature must not mean retirement of the family's underlying memories or history.
