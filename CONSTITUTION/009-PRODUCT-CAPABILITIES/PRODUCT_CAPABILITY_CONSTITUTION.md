# FEMC Product Capability Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define how FEMC capabilities are conceived, connected, evaluated, and evolved.

FEMC capabilities are expressions of the unified family-memory ecosystem. They must not become isolated mini-products.

## Capability Definition

A capability is a durable product ability that enables a meaningful family outcome.

A capability is not merely:

- a screen;
- a button;
- a database table;
- an API;
- a marketing feature;
- a temporary workflow.

## Capability Test

Every proposed capability must answer:

1. What family problem does it solve?
2. Which people or relationships does it serve?
3. Which canonical domain concepts does it use?
4. What family outcome does it improve?
5. How does it connect to the wider FEMC ecosystem?
6. What privacy and trust implications exist?
7. What historical data implications exist?
8. Can it evolve for ten years?
9. Is its complexity justified?

## Capability Composition

Capabilities should compose around canonical domain concepts.

For example:

An anniversary is an **Event**.

A photograph from the anniversary is **Media** associated with a **Memory**.

A family member's relationship to the people involved comes from the **Relationship** domain.

A celebration may use the event and memories.

AI may help discover previous anniversaries.

The calendar experience may present the event.

These are connected capabilities—not separate versions of the same information.

## Capability Ownership

Each capability must have a clearly assigned product owner or office.

Ownership of a capability does not grant ownership of the underlying canonical domain concept.

## Capability Lifecycle

```text
PROPOSED
   ↓
EVALUATED
   ↓
APPROVED
   ↓
DESIGNED
   ↓
IMPLEMENTED
   ↓
OPERATED
   ↓
EVOLVED / RETIRED
```

## Retirement

A capability may be retired when it no longer creates sufficient value.

Retirement must not automatically destroy the family information underlying that capability.

## Constitutional Principle

FEMC should grow by adding meaningful capabilities around a stable family model, not by continuously redefining the family model.
