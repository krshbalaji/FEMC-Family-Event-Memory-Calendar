# FEMC Strategic Product Principles

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Strategic Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## 1. Memory Over Mechanics

The calendar is a mechanism.

Family memory is the mission.

## 2. Relationships Give Context

A date without people is data.

A date connected to people, relationships, history, and memories becomes family context.

## 3. Preserve Before Enrich

The platform must first protect the family record before adding increasingly sophisticated intelligence.

## 4. Intelligence Must Remain Trustworthy

A useful answer that is wrong about family history is worse than an incomplete answer that admits uncertainty.

## 5. Human Agency

AI should make people more capable, not less human.

## 6. Quiet Technology

Technology should disappear into useful experiences where possible.

Families should not need to understand the architecture to benefit from it.

## 7. No Engagement Addiction

FEMC should not optimize for attention at the expense of family life.

A successful session may end with the family leaving the application to spend time together.

## 8. Generational Design

Every important capability should be evaluated for its value to people who may use the family record years later.

## 9. Interoperability

Family information should remain understandable beyond the current FEMC implementation.

## 10. Simplicity at the Surface

Internal architecture may become sophisticated as scale demands it.

The family experience should remain understandable.

## 11. Evolution Without Amnesia

New technology must not require forgetting old family history.

## 12. Technology Neutrality

Do not commit the product's future to today's fashionable technology.

## 13. Trust Compounds

Every predictable privacy-preserving interaction strengthens long-term trust.

Every unexplained or careless use of family information weakens it.

## 14. The Family Is the Customer

Analytics, AI, integrations, and infrastructure exist to serve the family outcome.

## 15. The Ten-Year Question

Before adding a major capability, ask:

> Will this make FEMC more valuable to a family ten years from now?
