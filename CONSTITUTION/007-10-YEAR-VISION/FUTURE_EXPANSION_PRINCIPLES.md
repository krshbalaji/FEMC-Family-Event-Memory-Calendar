# FEMC Future Expansion Principles

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Strategic Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define how FEMC may expand without losing its identity.

## Expansion Rule 1 — Extend the Family Context

New capabilities should connect naturally to existing family concepts.

## Expansion Rule 2 — Avoid Feature Islands

A new capability should identify its relationships with:

- people;
- relationships;
- events;
- memories;
- time;
- media;
- communication;
- legacy.

If it cannot connect meaningfully, its place in FEMC should be questioned.

## Expansion Rule 3 — Preserve Core Simplicity

New capability does not automatically mean new core domain.

## Expansion Rule 4 — Respect Privacy

Every expansion must identify new data exposure and trust implications before adoption.

## Expansion Rule 5 — Preserve Portability

New information should remain exportable and understandable where practical.

## Expansion Rule 6 — Design for Optionality

Families should not be forced to adopt every future capability.

The platform should remain useful even when advanced features are declined.

## Potential Future Areas

The following are examples for exploration, not commitments:

- Family traditions
- Family recipes
- Family places
- Family stories
- Family documents
- Family achievements
- Family projects
- Genealogy extensions
- Cultural archives
- Family learning
- Family travel memories
- Generational knowledge

## Expansion Test

A proposed future area should answer:

1. Does it strengthen family memory or connection?
2. Does it fit the canonical domain language?
3. Can it coexist with existing family history?
4. Does it preserve user control?
5. Does it introduce justified complexity?
6. Can it survive technology change?

## Final Principle

FEMC should become broader by becoming deeper in family meaning, not merely by accumulating features.
