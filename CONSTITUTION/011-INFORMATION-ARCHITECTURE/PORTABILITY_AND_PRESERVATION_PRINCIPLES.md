# FEMC Portability and Preservation Principles

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Strategic Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Ensure family information can survive technology changes and remain meaningful over time.

## 1. Portable Meaning

Export should preserve meaning, not merely produce a dump of technical records.

## 2. Stable Identity

Important family entities should have durable identifiers that can survive migrations.

## 3. Metadata Matters

Preserve relevant:

- dates;
- relationships;
- provenance;
- descriptions;
- associations;
- history;
- media metadata.

## 4. Media Integrity

Preservation must consider the relationship between media and the memory or event it represents.

A photograph without context may lose much of its historical value.

## 5. Human-Readable Preservation

Where practical, families should have access to understandable representations of their history in addition to machine-oriented exports.

## 6. Migration

Future migrations should be capable of preserving canonical meaning even when implementation technology changes.

## 7. Provider Independence

No external provider should become the only practical location in which a family's information remains meaningful.

## 8. Archive

Archival states must remain distinguishable from active information.

## 9. Recovery

Preservation includes the ability to recover from:

- accidental deletion;
- corruption;
- migration failure;
- provider failure;
- technology replacement.

## 10. Ten-Year Preservation Test

Ask:

> Could a family understand its memories if the original application no longer existed?

The architecture should progressively move toward yes.
