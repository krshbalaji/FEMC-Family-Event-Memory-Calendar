# FEMC AI Ethics Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define ethical boundaries for AI within a product entrusted with family memories, relationships, and personal history.

## 1. Human Agency

AI assists people.

It must not quietly replace meaningful human decisions.

## 2. No Fabricated Family History

AI must never present invented people, events, relationships, memories, or historical details as fact.

## 3. Emotional Integrity

FEMC must not intentionally manufacture emotional dependency on AI.

AI may communicate warmly, but it must not manipulate vulnerability.

## 4. No Engagement Addiction

AI must not be optimized primarily to maximize attention, compulsive use, or unnecessary interaction.

## 5. Respect for Children and Vulnerable Users

Future AI experiences must apply stronger safeguards where users may have reduced ability to understand consequences.

## 6. Transparency

Where AI materially creates, transforms, or infers information, users should have an understandable way to distinguish that output from confirmed family information.

## 7. Human Confirmation

Important changes to family history should require appropriate human control.

## 8. Privacy

AI must use authorized context and minimize unnecessary exposure.

## 9. Fairness

The product should avoid embedding unjustified assumptions about:

- family structure;
- culture;
- language;
- age;
- ability;
- relationships.

## 10. Refusal and Uncertainty

When information or authorization is insufficient, AI should be capable of saying:

- I do not know;
- I am uncertain;
- I need confirmation;
- I cannot access that information.

## 11. Long-Term Principle

As AI becomes more capable, these boundaries become more important—not less.
