# FEMC Architectural Evolution Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define how FEMC architecture may evolve over many years without losing its constitutional identity.

## 1. Evolution Is Expected

The architecture will change.

The family mission and canonical meaning should change much more slowly.

## 2. Preserve Stable Concepts

Future implementations should preserve stable concepts such as:

- person;
- relationship;
- family context;
- event;
- memory;
- media;
- legacy;
- permission.

## 3. Replace Technology, Not Meaning

A database, cloud platform, AI provider, interface, or implementation language may eventually be replaced.

Such replacement must not require redefining family meaning.

## 4. Evidence-Based Architecture

Major architectural changes should be driven by:

- real requirements;
- scale evidence;
- reliability needs;
- security;
- privacy;
- maintainability;
- cost;
- future compatibility.

## 5. Avoid Architectural Fashion

Technology should not be introduced simply because it is popular.

## 6. Reversible Decisions

Where uncertainty is high, prefer architectures that preserve future options.

## 7. Migration as a First-Class Concern

Significant architectural changes should consider migration before adoption.

## 8. Institutional Memory

Architecture decisions must preserve rationale and consequences.

## 9. No Silent Drift

Implementation must not gradually redefine the product constitution without explicit governance.

## 10. Ten-Year Principle

The architecture should be capable of becoming radically different internally while remaining recognizably FEMC to a family.
