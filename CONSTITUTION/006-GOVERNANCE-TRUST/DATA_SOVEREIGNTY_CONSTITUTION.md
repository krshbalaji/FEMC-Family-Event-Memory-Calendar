# FEMC Data Sovereignty Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define FEMC's long-term approach to ownership, portability, preservation, and control of family information.

## Sovereignty Principle

FEMC must not become a permanent prison for family history.

Families should retain meaningful control over their information.

## Canonical Family Data

The canonical family record belongs conceptually to the FEMC domain, not to:

- a cloud vendor;
- an AI provider;
- a database engine;
- a media storage service;
- an analytics provider.

Implementation systems are custodians and processors of the product's information.

## Portability

FEMC should support practical export and migration.

Exports should preserve, where feasible:

- people;
- relationships;
- events;
- memories;
- media references;
- metadata;
- dates;
- provenance;
- permissions-related information where appropriate.

## Preservation

Long-term preservation requires consideration of:

- readable formats;
- stable identifiers;
- metadata;
- media integrity;
- migration;
- archival;
- recovery.

## Vendor Independence

Provider-specific representations should not become the only meaningful representation of family information.

## Deletion

Deletion must be deliberate and governed.

Where information has dependencies, the system should make consequences understandable before destructive action.

## Data Integrity

A migration, export, import, or provider change must not silently alter historical meaning.

## Ten-Year Test

Ask:

> If the current technology provider disappeared tomorrow, could FEMC preserve the family's story and continue evolving?

The architecture should progressively move toward a responsible "yes."
