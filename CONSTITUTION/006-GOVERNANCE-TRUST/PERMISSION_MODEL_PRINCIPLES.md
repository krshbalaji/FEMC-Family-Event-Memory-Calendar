# FEMC Permission Model Principles

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define conceptual principles for access control without prescribing an implementation technology.

## Principle 1 — Permission Is Contextual

Access may depend on:

- person;
- family;
- relationship;
- resource;
- action;
- sharing context;
- time;
- purpose.

## Principle 2 — Visibility and Ownership Are Different

A person being able to see a memory does not necessarily mean they own or may modify it.

## Principle 3 — Least Privilege

People and systems should receive only the access necessary for the intended activity.

## Principle 4 — Explicit Sharing

Private information should not become shared merely because another feature can technically access it.

## Principle 5 — Family Complexity

The model must support different family structures and changing relationships.

It must not assume that one hierarchy or one household equals the complete family.

## Principle 6 — Historical Context

Permissions may change over time.

Changes must not automatically rewrite the historical meaning of previous family events or memories.

## Principle 7 — AI Permissions

AI access must be governed by the same trust principles.

A user asking an AI question does not automatically grant unrestricted access to all family information.

## Principle 8 — Administrative Power

Administrative authority must be carefully bounded.

System administration must not be confused with ownership of family memories.

## Principle 9 — Understandable Consequences

Important sharing or access decisions should communicate meaningful consequences before they take effect.

## Principle 10 — Future Evolution

The conceptual permission model must be capable of supporting future:

- family roles;
- guardianship;
- legacy access;
- delegated access;
- temporary sharing;
- emergency access;
- generational transition.

Implementation details belong to the appropriate security and engineering offices.
