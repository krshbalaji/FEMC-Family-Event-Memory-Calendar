# FEMC Governance Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define the principles by which FEMC decisions, responsibilities, constitutional changes, and long-term stewardship are governed.

## 1. Human Stewardship

FEMC is a human-centered platform.

No automated system, AI system, vendor, or implementation component may become the ultimate authority over the product's constitutional purpose.

## 2. Separation of Responsibilities

Each FEMC office has defined authority.

An office may make decisions within its mandate but must not silently assume another office's responsibilities.

Cross-office decisions must be documented.

## 3. Product Constitution Supremacy

Feature decisions must remain consistent with the Product Constitution.

A feature must not override a constitutional principle merely because it is convenient, commercially attractive, technically fashionable, or temporarily popular.

## 4. Evidence-Based Evolution

Major architectural or product changes should be supported by appropriate evidence such as:

- user need;
- operational evidence;
- security analysis;
- privacy analysis;
- scalability analysis;
- technical evidence;
- documented trade-offs.

## 5. Decision Records

Important decisions must preserve:

- decision;
- context;
- alternatives considered;
- rationale;
- consequences;
- owner;
- date;
- status.

The purpose is institutional memory, not bureaucracy.

## 6. Reversibility

Where uncertainty is high, FEMC should prefer decisions that preserve future options.

Irreversible decisions require stronger justification.

## 7. Transparency

Material product behavior should be understandable to the people affected by it.

## 8. Long-Term Stewardship

Governance must consider the possibility that:

- technology providers change;
- models change;
- teams change;
- leadership changes;
- product requirements evolve;
- families depend on FEMC for decades.

The architecture and documentation must therefore preserve institutional continuity.

## 9. Constitutional Change

Constitutional changes must be deliberate, documented, versioned, and reviewed for downstream impact.

A change to one constitutional document must trigger consideration of dependent documents.

## 10. Governance Goal

Governance exists to protect FEMC's mission, not to slow responsible progress.
