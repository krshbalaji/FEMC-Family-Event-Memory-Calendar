# FEMC Privacy Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Establish privacy as a foundational product property of FEMC.

## Core Principle

Family information is entrusted to FEMC.

Trust must be earned through predictable, respectful, privacy-preserving behavior.

## Privacy Principles

### Data Minimization

Collect and process what is reasonably necessary for the intended purpose.

### Purpose Limitation

Information collected for one purpose must not automatically become available for unrelated purposes.

### Contextual Access

Access should depend on the family context, permission, relationship, and intended action.

### User Control

Users should have meaningful control over:

- visibility;
- sharing;
- access;
- exports;
- deletion where appropriate;
- AI usage;
- connected services.

### Privacy by Default

Sensitive family information should begin from restrictive, privacy-preserving defaults where practical.

### Transparency

People should be able to understand what information is being used and why.

### Vendor Boundaries

External providers must not receive broader family context merely because integration is technically possible.

### AI Privacy

AI processing must respect the same privacy expectations as the rest of FEMC.

## Special Consideration

Family data may include information about people who are not active users.

FEMC must therefore avoid assuming that the absence of an account means the absence of privacy interests.

## Privacy and Memory

Privacy must coexist with preservation.

Long-term memory does not justify unlimited visibility.

A memory can be preserved while access remains controlled.

## Constitutional Rule

Convenience is not sufficient justification for weakening family privacy.
