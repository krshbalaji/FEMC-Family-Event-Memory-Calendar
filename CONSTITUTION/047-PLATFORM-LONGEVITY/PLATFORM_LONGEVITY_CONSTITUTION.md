# FEMC Platform Longevity Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define the principles required for FEMC to remain maintainable, understandable, and trustworthy over decades.

## 1. Long Life Is a Design Requirement

FEMC should be designed with the expectation that family information may outlive the original product team.

## 2. Stable Foundations

Core domain concepts should remain more stable than implementation technologies.

## 3. Maintainability

Architecture should favor systems that future teams can understand and safely change.

## 4. Institutional Memory

Important decisions, assumptions, and trade-offs must be preserved in documentation.

## 5. Dependency Discipline

Critical capabilities should avoid unnecessary dependence on providers that may disappear.

## 6. Technology Renewal

Infrastructure and implementation technology may be replaced progressively.

## 7. Data Preservation

Technology renewal must preserve family meaning and historical integrity.

## 8. Team Continuity

The repository should provide enough context for a new team to understand why FEMC works the way it does.

## 9. Economic Sustainability

Long-term viability includes sustainable operating cost.

## 10. Principle

FEMC should be capable of surviving its original implementation, technology stack, and team.
