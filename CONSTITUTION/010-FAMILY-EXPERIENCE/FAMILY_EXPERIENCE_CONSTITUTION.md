# FEMC Family Experience Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Constitutional Foundation  
**Owner:** Chief Product Architect

## Purpose

Define the experience philosophy of FEMC so that the product remains a living family space rather than becoming a collection of administrative screens.

## 1. Family Context First

The experience should begin from the family's context rather than from isolated features.

A person should be able to move naturally between:

- people;
- relationships;
- events;
- memories;
- media;
- celebrations;
- communication;
- family history.

## 2. Multiple Entry Points, One Family Reality

Families may enter FEMC through a calendar, memory, person, album, conversation, notification, or another future interface.

The entry point may differ.

The underlying family context must remain coherent.

## 3. Memory Should Be Discoverable

Important memories should not disappear simply because they were created in the past.

Discovery may happen through:

- people;
- relationships;
- events;
- time;
- albums;
- places;
- stories;
- future intelligent search.

Discovery must respect permission.

## 4. The Calendar Is a View

The calendar is one way to understand family time.

It is not the owner of the event domain.

Events should remain meaningful outside the calendar view.

## 5. The Family Tree Is a Context

The family tree is not merely a diagram.

Relationships should provide context for events, memories, celebrations, and legacy.

## 6. Human Before Interface

The product should reduce unnecessary interaction.

Not every family activity needs an app workflow.

A successful FEMC experience may help a family quickly remember something and then return to real life.

## 7. Emotional Respect

FEMC may support emotionally meaningful experiences, but it must not manipulate emotion.

Avoid:

- artificial urgency;
- guilt-driven engagement;
- addictive interaction patterns;
- fabricated emotional claims.

## 8. Generational Accessibility

The experience must consider:

- children;
- parents;
- grandparents;
- older adults;
- users with different technical abilities;
- multilingual families;
- different accessibility needs.

## 9. Progressive Complexity

Basic family tasks should remain understandable.

Advanced capabilities should appear when useful rather than forcing every family member to understand the complete platform.

## 10. AI as an Invisible Helper When Appropriate

AI may reduce friction without becoming the center of attention.

Users should be able to understand when AI has materially generated, inferred, or transformed information.

## 11. Trust Is Experienced

Privacy and permission are not only technical controls.

The experience should make users feel that family information is being handled predictably and respectfully.

## 12. Experience Evolution

Future interfaces may include:

- web;
- mobile;
- voice;
- conversational interaction;
- wearable or ambient interfaces;
- future interaction technologies.

The family model must survive changes in interface.

## Constitutional Test

Ask:

> Does this experience help a family live, remember, connect, or preserve—rather than merely operate software?

If not, reconsider the design.
